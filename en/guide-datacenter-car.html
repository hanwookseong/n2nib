Data Centre Contractors' All Risks (CAR)
Expected risks and the extensions you need

Data centre construction is dominated by power and cooling plant rather than the building itself, and is often carried out inside a live campus. This page sets out what CAR covers and which extensions a data centre project needs.

Written by Hanwook Seong — ACIU · Licensed insurance broker, FSS Reg. No. 2026-012201
Last updated 2026-11-08 · General explanation based on wording structures and insurers' materials; actual cover depends on product, wording and underwriting. · 한국어

KEY POINTS

  • CAR covers physical damage to the works and third-party liability arising from the works under one policy.
  • The main exposures are installing and energising switchgear, generators and chillers, and expansion work inside a live facility.
  • The existing building and running plant are not part of the works, so a separate surrounding-property sum insured is needed.
  • Loss from late completion is considered under a delay in start-up (DSU) extension — only delay caused by an insured loss, not delay as such.
  • Where plant is a large share, compare splitting it into erection all risks (EAR) or combining it in one policy.

03

Basic cover and main exclusions

What it covers

  • Physical loss or damage to the works (permanent and temporary works, materials on site) from sudden and unforeseen events
  • Debris removal (if a sum insured is set)
  • Liability for third-party injury and property damage caused by the works

Main exclusions

  • Defective design, material or manufacture as such
  • Delay, penalties and consequential loss (without an extension)
  • Injury to employees of firms connected with the works (employers' liability)
  • Wear, corrosion and gradual deterioration
  • Existing property in operation (without an extension)

05

Policyholder and insureds

  • The policyholder is usually the owner or main contractor. Include subcontractors and plant suppliers as insureds with a mutual waiver of subrogation.
  • The sum insured is the full contract value plus owner-supplied materials; underinsurance can reduce claims proportionately.
  • If plant is supplied and installed under separate contracts, document which policy covers which plant (the CAR/EAR boundary).

Scenarios

Wrong isolation during expansion

During cabling next to a live data hall, the wrong breaker is opened and some plant stops and is damaged.

Likely position — Plant damage may be covered where a surrounding-property extension applies. Loss from service interruption falls under separate cover such as business interruption. (standard textbook scenario)

Frequently asked questions

Do we need CAR or EAR for a data centre?

CAR is the basis where building and civil works dominate; EAR where installation and testing of plant dominate. Where both are present, combine them in one policy or set a clear boundary.

What should we watch when expanding inside a live data centre?

The running building and plant are not part of the works, so set a surrounding-property sum insured. Segregation of work areas and hot-work control are key underwriting information.

Is late completion covered?

Delay caused by an insured loss can be considered under a DSU extension. Delay without an insured loss, and penalties, are not covered.
