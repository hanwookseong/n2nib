GUIDE · DATA CENTRES

Data Centre Risks and Insurance by Lifecycle Stage
From construction to installation and operation

A data centre changes hands between insurance policies as the building is constructed, high-value servers are delivered, installed and tested, and the facility is then operated and maintained for years. This guide sets out the risks at each stage, the cover that responds, and the gaps that tend to appear between stages.

Written by Hanwook Seong — ACIU · Licensed insurance broker, FSS Reg. No. 2026-012201
Last updated 2026-11-08 · Based on Korean erection all risks (EAR) and contractors' all risks (CAR) wording structures, the Korean Network Act and insurers' product materials · Premiums, limits and acceptance are determined by the insurers' underwriting.
Request a review한국어

KEY POINTS

  • Construction is covered mainly by contractors' all risks (CAR); installation and testing of servers and plant by erection all risks (EAR); operation after handover by property, electronic equipment and business interruption cover.
  • In operation, liability to others sits with general liability (CGL), service errors with technology E&O, and hacking with cyber cover.
  • Gaps usually appear where one stage hands over to the next: unloading, the test period, handover, and damage to equipment in a maintenance contractor's care.
  • A contractor's liability policy generally does not cover damage to the equipment being worked on. The owner's property cover, a waiver of subrogation and contractual liability caps need to be designed together.
  • Downtime without physical damage is often outside property and BI cover, so contract (SLA) terms come first.

03

Cover by stage in detail

① Contractors' all risks (CAR)

While the building and M&E systems are built

What it covers

  • Physical loss or damage to the works from sudden and unforeseen events such as fire, explosion, collapse or natural perils
  • Liability for third-party injury or property damage caused by the works
  • Extensions to consider: delay in start-up (DSU) and existing surrounding property

Usually arranged by

The owner or main contractor. For expansion or refurbishment inside a live data centre, the existing building and running plant become surrounding property and need their own sum insured.

Product details →

② Cargo and transit insurance

Until the equipment reaches the site

What it covers

  • Damage to or loss of equipment in air, sea or inland transit
  • Where cover ends depends on the policy and delivery terms; check separately whether unloading and movement within the site are included

Usually arranged by

The supplier or importer — who bears transit risk depends on the Incoterms.

Product details →

③ Erection all risks (EAR)

While servers and plant are delivered, installed and tested

What it covers

  • Physical loss or damage to the equipment being installed, from sudden and unforeseen events during site storage, moving, installation and testing (fire, theft, drops in handling, erection faults, testing incidents)
  • Third-party liability arising from the installation work
  • Extensions to consider: extended test period, cover after partial handover, damage caused by a defect to other parts (LEG), serial-loss terms, extra charges for express freight, surrounding property, advance loss of profits (ALOP)

Usually arranged by

Often the installer as policyholder, with the equipment supplier, owner and other interested parties as co-insureds, together with a mutual waiver of subrogation.

Example — a connection leaks while coolant is being filled into a liquid-cooling loop, damaging equipment below (standard textbook scenario) · Product details →

④ Operational property cover — property, EEI and BI

Protecting the building, plant and servers after handover

What it covers

  • Property / package: physical damage to building, plant and servers from fire, explosion, natural perils, etc.
  • Electronic equipment and machinery: electrical and mechanical breakdown of servers, UPS and cooling plant — often excluded under a standard fire policy
  • Business interruption: loss of profit and standing charges while operations are interrupted by insured physical damage

Usually arranged by

The building and plant owner, the operator, and server owners. An operator's policy does not automatically cover tenants' or customers' equipment, so equipment owners should check their own cover.

Property all risks → · Business interruption → · Machinery breakdown →

⑤ Operational liability — CGL, tech E&O, cyber, statutory cover

What you may owe to others

What it covers

  • General liability (CGL): third-party injury or property damage from premises and operations, and completed operations
  • Technology E&O: financial loss to customers from errors in operation or maintenance services
  • Cyber: liability and response costs from hacking and ransomware
  • Statutory: under Article 46(2) of the Korean Network Act, operators of integrated information and communications facilities must insure against damage from loss, damage or operational failure of the facility, as set by Presidential Decree

Usually arranged by

Operators, maintenance contractors and managed service providers — customer contracts often specify cover and limits.

CGL → · Tech E&O → · Cyber →

⑥ Employers' liability

Injury to construction, installation and operations staff

What it covers

  • Employers' liability above the statutory industrial accident scheme
  • EAR and CAR liability sections usually exclude injury to employees of firms connected with the works, so each employer needs its own cover

Usually arranged by

Each employer — main contractor, installer, subcontractors and operator.

04

Where gaps appear

G1 Unloading — where does cargo cover end?

EAR usually starts immediately after unloading at site. If no one checks whether the cargo policy covers unloading and movement within the site (for example from the loading bay to a storage room), an incident in that window may fall under neither policy. Starting EAR on completion of unloading is one solution.

G2 Test period — limits and extensions

EAR testing cover is often limited by the wording (for example four weeks from the start of testing). High-density servers are tested with loads raised step by step, which can take longer. Submit the test plan in advance and agree an extension in writing. Defects in design, material or manufacture are excluded as such; damage they cause to other parts and repeated losses from the same cause (serial losses) are points to negotiate.

G3 Handover — lot-by-lot transfer

When equipment is handed over in lots, each lot leaves EAR on the day it is handed over. Check that the owner's property cover (including EEI and BI) starts the same day, or whether partial-operation cover is needed.

G4 Damage during maintenance — care, custody or control

If a maintenance contractor damages a customer's server after handover, the contractor's liability policy generally excludes damage to property being worked on. A waiver of subrogation (or additional-insured status) for the contractor under the owner's property cover, plus liability caps and a consequential-loss exclusion in the contract, narrow the gap.

Downtime without physical damage

Property and BI cover require physical damage, so outages without equipment damage — grid failure or software causes — are often outside cover. SLA credits are often designed as fee reductions and are commonly not covered under standard liability wordings either. SLA guide (Korean) →

Frequently asked questions

Is contractors' all risks enough when building a data centre?

CAR is the main cover for constructing the building and systems, but the stage in which high-value servers and plant are delivered, installed and tested is often insured separately under EAR. After handover, cover must continue under property, electronic equipment and business interruption insurance.

When does EAR start and end?

Usually immediately after unloading at site or at the start of work, ending at handover or on completion of the first test run, whichever is earlier. Testing is often limited by the wording, and cover for a part ends when that part is handed over. The exact period is set by the policy and its extensions.

If a maintenance contractor damages a server, does its liability policy pay?

Damage to property being worked on is generally excluded under a liability policy. A waiver of subrogation in favour of the contractor under the equipment owner's property cover, together with a contractual liability cap, can narrow the gap.

Must data centre operators carry insurance in Korea?

Article 46(2) of the Act on Promotion of Information and Communications Network Utilization and Information Protection requires operators of integrated information and communications facilities to insure against damage from loss, damage or operational failure of the facility, as set by Presidential Decree. Whether a business is subject to it, and the amounts, should be checked against the law.

How are premiums set?

Insurers consider equipment and plant values, building construction and fire protection, power and cooling redundancy, work methods, the test plan, loss history, limits and deductibles. Premiums and terms are confirmed only after underwriting.
