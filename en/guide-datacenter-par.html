Data Centre Property All Risks (PAR)
Property, electronic equipment and BI in operation

After handover, the building, power and cooling plant and the servers move to the owner's property insurance. A fire policy alone tends to leave electrical and mechanical breakdown and business interruption uncovered.

Written by Hanwook Seong — ACIU · Licensed insurance broker, FSS Reg. No. 2026-012201
Last updated 2026-11-08 · General explanation based on wording structures and insurers' materials; actual cover depends on product, wording and underwriting. · 한국어

KEY POINTS

  • Property all risks (PAR) broadly covers physical damage from sudden and unforeseen events to buildings, plant and servers.
  • Many data centre incidents are electrical or mechanical breakdowns of UPS, transformers and cooling plant, which standard fire policies often exclude.
  • Business interruption (BI) pays for loss while operations are interrupted by insured physical damage — not for interruptions without physical damage.
  • Long plant lead times mean the indemnity period should be set on the replacement timeline.
  • Where tenants and maintenance contractors are involved, set the waiver of subrogation and customers' property cover together.

03

Basic cover and main exclusions

What it covers

  • Physical loss or damage to buildings, plant, contents and servers from sudden and unforeseen events
  • Fire, explosion, natural perils, theft, leakage, etc. (all risks, subject to exclusions)
  • By extension, machinery breakdown, electronic equipment and business interruption

Main exclusions

  • Damage to a unit itself from its own electrical or mechanical breakdown (without an extension)
  • Wear, corrosion and gradual deterioration
  • Loss of data or software, cyber events
  • Interruption without physical damage
  • Wilful acts, war, nuclear risks

If you use a package policy: four things to check

Large and foreign-invested operators often combine property all risks, machinery breakdown, business interruption and liability in one package policy. The structure can work for a data centre in operation, but these four points need to be checked against the policy and its endorsements.

  1. Where servers and IT equipment are covered — The property section of a package typically excludes breakdown and short-circuit of electronic equipment, and the machinery breakdown section covers only items entered in its schedule. Whether servers can be scheduled there is an underwriting decision. If you also need data media reinstatement, increased cost of working (ICOW) and damage caused by air-conditioning failure, a separate electronic equipment policy (EEI) fits the structure better.
  2. Which cyber and data exclusion applies — Property policies carry a cyber and data exclusion. Broad versions (for example LMA5401) define failure or unavailability of a computer system as a cyber incident, which can lead to disputes over losses arising from server failure. Check which clause is attached and whether a limited form that writes back physical damage from fire or explosion can be agreed.
  3. UPS batteries and cables — Some machinery breakdown sections exclude batteries, connecting wires and cables, and operating media such as refrigerants. UPS batteries and power cabling are a large share of data centre assets, so confirm whether they are scheduled.
  4. Equipment being installed or commissioned — Package policies exclude property under erection, testing or commissioning, and machinery damage before performance testing is completed. Cover new racks and expansions under EAR and move them to the package at handover.

This is a general explanation based on clause structures commonly found in Korean package policy wordings. Actual cover and endorsements depend on the insurer, product and policy, and are confirmed after underwriting.

Policyholder and insureds

  • The policyholder is the building and plant owner or the operator. Where servers have a different owner, make clear whose policy covers them.
  • Align the start of this policy with the end of the EAR for each lot (handover date).

Frequently asked questions

Does a fire policy cover data centre plant?

Fire damage is covered, but electrical breakdown of transformers or UPS units is often excluded. Consider machinery breakdown and electronic equipment extensions.

Is an outage from a power cut covered by BI?

BI requires physical damage. An outage caused by external power failure without equipment damage is often not covered unless a utility interruption extension applies.

Are tenants' servers covered by the operator's policy?

Not automatically. Add customers' property cover or set out in the contract that tenants insure their own equipment.

Can the machinery breakdown section of a package policy replace electronic equipment insurance (EEI)?

They overlap in part but are not interchangeable. The machinery breakdown section covers breakdown of scheduled items and does not include data media reinstatement or increased cost of working. Whether servers can be scheduled is decided at underwriting, and data centre IT equipment is often insured under a separate EEI.
