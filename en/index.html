The five things a broker does.

Korea's non-life market has complex wordings, endorsements and exclusions. An insurance broker (N2N Insurance Brokerage) is an independent advisor who compares insurers' wordings, negotiates, handles claims and reviews renewals on the client's side. An insurer's sales force represents the insurer; a broker, under Article 89 of the Insurance Business Act, represents the client.

Frequently asked questions

The 4 most common questions before using a broker

What is an insurance broker?

A professional registered under Article 89 of the Insurance Business Act who compares and intermediates among several insurers on the client's (the company's) behalf. Unlike an agency, which acts for the insurer, a broker stands on the client's side.

Who pays the brokerage fee?

The insurer pays it; it is not added on top of the premium you pay (in compliance with Article 98 of the Insurance Business Act — prohibition of special benefits).

Why compare 6 insurers?

Underwriting appetite, rates and wordings differ by insurer depending on industry, line of cover and prior loss history. We review the 6 together to find the most suitable cover and terms for your company.

What happens after I request a consultation?

① Review of your company and risk → ② underwriting check across 6 insurers → ③ results returned (avg. 1 business day) → ④ you compare and choose → ⑤ application and certificate issue.
