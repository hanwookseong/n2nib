Private Academy Liability Insurance

Insurance taken out by academy operators under the Act on the Establishment and Operation of Private Teaching Institutes and Extracurricular Lessons. It covers bodily and property loss to students and staff from accidents while running the academy and its facilities.

Overview

Private academy liability insurance compensates loss the insured suffers by incurring legal liability when an accidental incident — arising from the academy facilities it owns, uses or manages, and from work according to their use — injures another person (a student or visitor) or damages their property.

Facilities under the Act — teaching rooms, entrance-exam academies, art, music and sports academies, reading rooms and study cafés — are recommended to insure, and some local authorities require proof of cover at registration or reporting.

Key features

  • 01
    Combined academy-facility cover

    Accidents in any space in the academy — classrooms, practice rooms, corridors, stairs.

  • 02
    Instructor-negligence cover

    Accidental incidents caused by instructors or teachers during lessons.

  • 03
    No-fault on-site medical cover

    Treatment costs even for a student's own carelessness, up to a set limit.

  • 04
    Experiment / practice / field-learning extension

    Extension for incidents during practice in art, science, sport and more.

Who needs it

  • 01
    Entrance-exam & cram academies

    Primary, middle and high-school exam and supplementary academies.

  • 02
    Arts & sports academies

    Art, music, taekwondo, ballet, yoga and other arts/sports facilities.

  • 03
    Skills & certification academies

    Lifelong-learning academies — computing, languages, certifications.

  • 04
    Reading rooms & study cafés

    Reading-room, study-café and study-room operators.

Notable claim examples

📍

Classroom collision — broken nose

A case where, just after class, a student rising while grabbing a bag struck their nose on the back of a classmate's head, causing a nosebleed and a broken nose. Part of the academy's safety-management responsibility was recognised.

📍

Art-academy glue-gun burn

A case where a first-grader gluing a robot leg at an art academy was burned when melted glue dripped onto their hand. The academy immediately gave first aid with burn spray and ointment, and the child recovered without scarring after hospital treatment.

📍

Reading-room chair leg breaking — student fall

A case where a middle-school student stretching while studying in a reading room fell backward as the chair leg broke. The facility's responsibility for poor maintenance of an old chair was recognised and treatment costs were paid.

※ The above are generalised examples of industry claims; actual cover outcomes may differ according to the wording and application terms.

Main losses covered

  • Damages for bodily injury to students or visitors from an in-academy accident
  • Property loss to students or visitors
  • On-site medical costs (no-fault treatment costs for incidents within the facility)
  • Negligence accidents by instructors or teachers in the course of duty
  • Emergency costs, litigation and legal fees for handling an incident

Endorsements (additional cover)

  • No-fault on-site medical-cost endorsement
  • Field-learning / external-excursion extension endorsement
  • Experiment / practice / materials-use incident extension
  • Instructor professional-liability endorsement
  • Food-liability endorsement (in-academy shop)

Losses not covered (main exclusions)

  • The academy head's (insured's) wilful acts or gross negligence
  • The workers'-comp area for academy staff (instructors, etc.) themselves
  • A student's clearly private conduct
  • Property damage to the academy facility itself (the facility-insurance area)
  • War, civil war and nuclear risk

Why the surrender value may be less than the premiums paid, or nil

The surrender value is the amount paid if the contract is cancelled early. Unlike bank savings, insurance combines risk protection and savings: part of the premium is paid out as claims to other policyholders who suffer accidents, and part covers the insurer's operating expenses, so the surrender value on early cancellation may be less than the premiums paid, or nil.

When you need academy liability insurance

Common risk patterns in academy operation — a five-scenario self-check

📋

Proof of cover required at local-authority registration/reporting

Some local authorities require proof of liability cover when an academy registers or reports. Confirm in advance that the cover suits your operation.

🤸

Running lessons with much physical activity — experiments, practice, sport

Physical-activity lessons such as art (glue guns, materials), science practice, sport and taekwondo carry a high risk of student injury. A practice-risk extension should be considered.

👶

Academies with a high share of minor students

Primary and middle-school students have a high frequency of accidental incidents such as collisions and falls, and the academy's safety-management and supervision responsibility tends to be broadly recognised.

🧑‍🏫

Academies with many instructors

Accidental incidents during instructors' teaching lead to the academy's vicarious liability, not just the individual instructor's.

🪑

Facility-centred operations such as reading rooms and study cafés

Injury to users from ageing seats and fixtures or facility defects can create owner/manager liability even in unstaffed operation.

A dispute pattern seen in the field

Disputes often arise where a non-academy facility is insured under “academy liability.” If a facility that is not an academy under the Act — a counselling centre, a vocational-training institution, a lifelong-learning facility — is classed as an academy, then in an accident the actual business differs from the business on the policy and a dispute over cover arises. Confirming at application the legal basis on which the facility operates — academy, lifelong-learning facility or vocational-training institution — and designing the matching liability cover is the starting point for reducing disputes.

Three things easily missed when buying academy liability insurance

The wording and structure points decision-makers most often overlook

  • 1

    Mis-classification — insuring a non-academy as an academy

    Counselling centres, vocational-training institutions, lifelong-learning facilities and driving academies are distinct from academies under the Act. Mis-applying the business type can affect not just the premium but cover itself in an accident, so insure based on the facility's legal basis.

  • 2

    Missing the practice / field-trip risk extension

    Art and science practice, physical activity and external field trips can leave gaps under base cover alone. Check that the experiment/practice/field-learning extension matching your lessons is included.

  • 3

    The scope of no-fault on-site medical cover

    An accident from a student's own carelessness may not establish the academy's legal liability, and the no-fault on-site medical endorsement supplements this within a set limit. The limit should be reviewed together.

Frequently asked questions

The questions decision-makers ask most when considering academy liability insurance

How do I confirm my facility is an “academy”?

Academies and teaching rooms registered under the Act are the subjects of academy liability insurance. Counselling centres, vocational-training institutions, lifelong-learning facilities and driving academies have a different legal basis and are usually designed under a separate liability cover. Confirming the business type by the facility's registration basis reduces cover disputes in an accident.

Are accidents an instructor causes in class covered for the academy?

An accidental incident an instructor causes while teaching leads to the academy's vicarious liability and is assessable for cover per the wording. Considering an instructor professional-liability endorsement can broaden the scope.

Is a student hurt by their own carelessness covered too?

An accident from a student's own carelessness may not establish the academy's legal liability. However, with a no-fault on-site medical endorsement, treatment costs for in-facility incidents can be covered up to a set limit regardless of fault.

Are accidents during art/science practice or field trips covered too?

Accidents during experiments, practice, materials use and external field trips can leave gaps under base cover alone, so they are covered via the practice / field-learning extension. Check at application that the endorsement matching your lessons is included.

Does this insurance handle damage to the academy building or fixtures themselves?

No. Academy liability insurance covers the legal liability for bodily and property loss to others (students, visitors). Damage to the academy facility itself falls under property insurance such as fire or comprehensive property cover.

How is the premium calculated?

The insurer calculates it based on the academy type, the number and age of students, facility area and structure, practice risk and past incident history. The exact premium and terms are confirmed after underwriting by insurers such as AIG, Chubb, DB, Hyundai, KB and Meritz.
