Overview

Article 20-2 of the Certified Architects Act and its Enforcement Decree make professional liability insurance mandatory or recommended for architectural firms performing design, supervision and similar work above a certain scale. This cover responds to loss to owners and third parties from design-document errors, inadequate structural review or deficient supervision.

When you need architects' professional liability insurance

Common risk patterns in design and supervision work — a five-scenario self-check

📐

Architectural firms that prepare building design documents

Errors in design documents or inadequate structural review can lead to building defects and create liability to owners and third parties.

🔍

When performing construction-stage supervision

If deficient supervision fails to detect a construction defect, the architect's professional liability may be established.

📋

When the scale of work triggers a requirement to insure

The Certified Architects Act and its Enforcement Decree make professional liability insurance mandatory or recommended for firms performing work above a certain scale.

🏗️

Firms that also perform structural review and technical services

Structural review and technical-service work bears directly on building safety, so negligence can cause large losses.

🏙️

When work has expanded into interior, urban or landscape design

The broader the scope of design work, the greater the claims exposure, so cover must be designed to the work performed.

A dispute pattern seen in the field

Architects' professional liability insurance covers loss caused to owners and third parties by negligence in the architect's “professional work” — design-document errors, inadequate structural review, deficient supervision. Two things are often misunderstood here. First, this cover is generally written on a claims-made basis, responding to claims made during the policy period, so a lapse can leave claims arising from past projects in a gap. Second, while the architect's design and supervision negligence is covered, defects arising from the contractor's construction itself, or the firm's own business disputes, are separate areas. The boundary of cover is exactly how far “design and supervision negligence” extends.

Frequently asked questions

The questions asked most when considering architects' professional liability insurance

Is architects' professional liability insurance mandatory?

Article 20-2 of the Certified Architects Act and its Enforcement Decree make professional liability insurance mandatory or recommended for architectural firms performing design and supervision work above a certain scale. The applicable criteria differ by the scale of work, so check against the relevant law.

What negligence is covered?

Bodily injury or property damage caused to owners and third parties by negligence in the architect's design and supervision work — design-document errors, inadequate structural review, deficient supervision — is what is covered.

Are the contractor's construction defects covered too?

Architects' professional liability insurance covers the architect's design and supervision negligence. Defects arising from the contractor's construction itself are a separate area, handled by construction-stage insurance and bonds.

Are claims from past projects covered too?

This cover is generally written on a claims-made basis. A claim arising from a past project can be covered if it is made during the policy period, but claims already known before inception are excluded, and it is important to keep cover in force without a lapse.

Are other services such as structural review and interior design covered too?

Expanding into structural review, interior or urban design changes the risk. Check at application that the policy's covered scope of work captures the work you actually perform.

How is the premium calculated?

The insurer calculates it based on firm size and staff, revenue, the type and scale of current projects, the limit, and past claims history. The exact premium and terms are confirmed after underwriting by insurers such as AIG, Chubb, DB, Hyundai, KB and Meritz.
