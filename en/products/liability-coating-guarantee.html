Overview

Coating guarantee liability insurance covers re-coating and legal liability costs when coating-film defects such as peeling, corrosion or discolouration appear within a guarantee period after a coating job is completed. For shipbuilding, offshore plant and large buildings the coating guarantee period runs long — typically 5 to 10 years — and this risk is hedged through insurance.

Coating fields that need coating guarantee liability

Peeling and corrosion risk by work environment — five coating categories

🏢

Building exterior coating

Curtain walls, exterior walls and rooftops. Exposed to UV, temperature swings and rainfall — a high long-term-guarantee-risk area.

🌉

Bridge & structure coating

Anti-rust and anti-fouling coating on bridge steelwork and transmission towers. The longest guarantee periods, from long-term ageing and corrosion risk.

🚢

Marine & ship coating

Seawater-corrosion-environment coating on ship hulls and offshore platforms. The guarantee specification needs precise design for marine variables.

🏭

Industrial facility coating

Anti-rust coating on plants, tanks and pipelines. A chemical-exposure environment that calls for review of out-of-specification exclusions.

⚡

Specialist coating

Specialist-environment coating for semiconductor clean-rooms, aerospace and precision parts. A field where a single guarantee claim can be large.

A dispute pattern seen in the field

A bridge-coating firm applied anti-rust coating to steelwork to a 10-year guarantee specification, but at the five-year mark peeling and corrosion were found in some sections. The client demanded re-coating for breach of the coating guarantee. The firm did hold an ordinary PL policy, but re-coating costs arising from a coating-guarantee breach differ from PL's third-party-damage scope, so applying the wording took time. Because the apportionment of liability shifts with the cause — workmanship responsibility, material defect or environmental variables — this case shows why combining a guarantee wording into the design is essential.

Three things easily missed when buying coating guarantee liability insurance

The wording and structure points decision-makers most often overlook

  • 1

    Guarantee period vs specification and environmental conditions

    The coating guarantee period is set on the premise of the coating specification and the work-environment conditions. If environmental variables outside the specification (typhoon, acid rain, seawater ingress) come into play, the guarantee wording may exclude the loss — so check in advance that the specified environmental conditions match the policy's exclusion definitions.

  • 2

    Environmental-variable exclusion clauses

    Natural disasters, out-of-specification chemical action, UV and temperature swings exceeding the specification range, and the user's negligent maintenance are typical exclusions. Exclusion definitions differ slightly by insurer, so comparing exclusion clauses by work environment is central at the application stage.

  • 3

    Construction liability vs material (paint) liability

    The apportionment shifts according to whether the cause is poor workmanship or a defect in the paint itself. The coating firm bears workmanship liability and the paint maker bears material PL liability, so without designing this in advance through an indemnity clause in the B2B supply contract, post-incident disputes can drag on.

Frequently asked questions

The questions decision-makers ask most when considering coating guarantee liability insurance

How is coating guarantee liability insurance different from ordinary PL?

Ordinary PL primarily covers third-party bodily injury or property damage caused by a defect in the product (the paint) itself. Coating guarantee liability covers the duty to make good on the coating workmanship and material guarantee — handling guarantee-breach events such as peeling, corrosion, discolouration or short durability. Coating firms commonly combine the two wordings.

How is the coating guarantee period set?

The guarantee period is set by the coating specification (exterior, bridge, marine, industrial), the paint specification, the work environment and the client's required specification. General building exteriors are usually 5–10 years; bridges and industrial facilities 10–20 years; marine coating varies with environmental conditions.

What kinds of exclusions apply?

Typical exclusions include natural disasters (typhoon, flood, earthquake), out-of-specification chemical action (acid rain, seawater ingress), unauthorised modification or re-coating, environmental variables exceeding the specification range (UV, temperature swings) and the user's negligent maintenance. The key is checking whether the coating specification's environmental conditions match the policy's exclusion definitions.

How are construction liability and material (paint) liability divided?

Whether peeling or corrosion stems from poor workmanship or from a defect in the paint itself determines the liability area. Usually the coating firm bears the workmanship liability and the paint maker bears the material PL liability; the division is often designed in advance through an indemnity clause in the B2B supply contract.

Can overseas work be covered?

The base wording usually covers work within Korean territory only. Overseas EPC/plant coating, export ships and offshore-structure coating need a separate territorial-extension endorsement or a structure combined with global PL; the extendable scope and additional premium differ by insurer.

How is the limit designed?

The limit is designed considering project scale, the maximum single-job area or contract value, and the cumulative risk over the guarantee period. For large single-project areas such as bridges, marine and plant work, per-project limit design or a master-wording combination is common.

How is the premium assessed?

The insurer assesses it on the coating specification (exterior, bridge, marine, industrial), the work environment, the guarantee period, turnover, past guarantee-claim history and any agreement with the paint maker. The exact premium and whether cover can be accepted are confirmed after underwriting by insurers such as AIG, Chubb, DB, KB, Meritz and Hyundai.
