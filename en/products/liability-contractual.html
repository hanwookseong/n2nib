Contractual Liability Insurance

Covers loss from a heightened indemnity obligation or failure to perform an undertaking under a contract. It specialises in the additional contractual liability that general liability insurance does not cover — hedging indemnity-clause risk in EPC, IT-outsourcing and service contracts.

Overview

Covers monetary loss from a heightened indemnity clause stated in a B2B contract, or from an SLA-breach indemnity obligation. It supplements the “contractually heightened liability” exclusion in general CGL and PL, and includes Indemnity-and-Indemnification cover.

Nullity of the contract

If the insured event has already occurred at the time the contract is made, the contract is void. However, where the contract is void due to the company's intent or negligence, or where the company knew or could have known of the nullity before acceptance yet did not refund the premium, the company refunds the premium with interest at the policy-loan rate published by the Korea Insurance Development Institute, compounded annually, for the period from the day after payment to the day of refund.

Cover start date

The company provides cover, in accordance with the policy, from the time it accepts the application and receives the first premium. Where the company accepts the application after receiving the first premium with it, cover also begins from the time the first premium was received.

Withdrawal of the application

  • The policyholder may withdraw the application within 15 daysof receiving the policy, in which case the premium paid is refunded. However, a contract more than 30 days after application (45 days where a policyholder aged 65+ contracted by telephone) cannot be withdrawn.
  • In addition, a medical-examination contract, a contract with a cover period of 90 days or less, guarantee insurance, statutory compulsory insurance, liability insurance under the Automobile Accident Compensation Act, or a commercial-insurance contract concluded by a professional financial consumer cannot be withdrawn.

Quality-assurance scheme

  • If, after application, the policyholder did not receive the policy wording and their copy of the application, was not given an explanation of the important contents of the wording, or did not sign the application by hand, they may cancel the contract within 3 monthsof the contract being formed.
  • In that case the premium already paid is refunded to the policyholder, with interest at the policy-loan rate compounded annually for the period the premium was held.

Why the surrender value may be less than the premiums paid, or nil

The surrender value is the amount paid if the contract is cancelled early. Unlike bank savings, insurance combines risk protection and savings: part of the premium is paid out as claims to other policyholders who suffer accidents, and part covers the insurer's operating expenses, so the surrender value on early cancellation may be less than the premiums paid, or nil.

Depositor protection

  • Separately, the aggregate accident-claim amount of that insurer's protected products is “KRW 100 million per person” protected.
  • (However, a policy whose policyholder and premium payer is a corporation is not protected.)

When you need contractual liability cover

Five patterns with high B2B-contract indemnity risk

🤝

B2B supply-contract indemnity

Indemnity clauses in the standard contracts of large buyers and customers. You may bear indirect and consequential loss as well as direct loss.

📊

SLA breach (service level)

SLA-breach penalties in IT, SaaS and outsourcing contracts. Usually a PL-excluded area, so separate cover should be considered.

🔑

Licence and technology-transfer contracts

Warranty and IP-infringement indemnity in technology-licence, OEM and ODM contracts. Consider designing together with IP liability.

📦

Purchase / delivery-deadline breach

Penalties for late delivery or sub-standard quality. Auto-trigger clauses may be excluded, so a wording review is essential.

🏗

EPC / plant owner contracts

Unlimited indemnity under an EPC contract with the owner. Designed together with construction and completed-operations wordings.

A dispute pattern seen in the field

A parts maker signing a B2B supply contract with a large electronics firm had a structure under which an indemnity clause made it bear all direct, indirect and consequential loss. A part defect halted the customer's finished-goods line for several days, and the customer claimed a large loss; but because general PL's core scope is third-party compensation, the heightened indemnity liability was close to an excluded area. With no separate contractual liability policy held, this case shows that combining PL with contractual liability is effectively the standard for B2B operators.

Three things easily missed when buying contractual liability cover

The wording and structure points decision-makers most often overlook

  • 1

    Indemnity scope can become effectively unlimited liability

    Indemnity clauses in B2B standard contracts often include indirect, consequential and punitive loss as well as direct loss, becoming effectively unlimited liability. Buying cover without estimating the contract-indemnity exposure can leave the policy limit short of the real risk.

  • 2

    Exclusion of auto-trigger penalty clauses

    Penalties that arise automatically on an SLA breach or late delivery trigger without proof of an incident, so they usually fall in an excluded area. Check in advance whether the contract's auto-trigger penalty clauses are covered in the wording.

  • 3

    Exclusion of wilful acts and gross negligence

    A contract breach from the operator's wilful act or gross negligence is usually excluded. A breach judged grossly negligent due to weak contract management can affect the cover assessment, so a systematic in-house contract-review process is also favourable at underwriting.

Frequently asked questions

The questions asked most when considering contractual liability cover

How does it differ from general PL?

General PL's core scope is third-party bodily-injury and property compensation from a product defect. Contractual liability covers the contractually heightened liability (indemnity, penalties, consequential loss, etc.) arising in B2B supply, SLA and licence contracts, supplementing what PL usually excludes. Combining PL with contractual liability is usual for B2B operators.

Is SLA-breach liability covered too?

Penalties or consequential loss from an SLA breach usually fall in a PL-excluded area, but they can be coverable under a contractual liability wording. However, auto-trigger penalty clauses or unlimited indemnity may be excluded, so a contract review and wording comparison are needed at application.

How is the limit for heightened indemnity liability designed?

B2B-contract indemnity often includes indirect, consequential and punitive loss as well as direct loss, becoming an effectively unlimited liability risk. The limit is designed considering the maximum contract-indemnity exposure per counterparty, revenue share and single-deal size, and the limit-design options differ by insurer.

What exclusions apply?

Usual exclusions include the operator's wilful act or gross negligence, auto-trigger penalty/penalty clauses (varying greatly by wording), criminal-penalty costs, administrative sanctions, and political risk, war and terrorism. Checking in advance how far the wording covers the contract indemnity is key.

Is licence/IP infringement within cover?

Indemnity liability for IP infringement — patent, trademark, copyright — is usually separated into a dedicated IP-liability area. However, a contractual liability wording can cover some licence-breach indemnity, so designing IP liability and contractual liability together should be considered.

How are the limit and deductible designed?

The limit is designed considering the maximum indemnity exposure per counterparty, revenue share and single-contract size. The deductible is a means to reduce small indemnity claims and adjust the premium, and the limit/deductible combinations differ widely by insurer.

How is the premium calculated?

The insurer calculates it based on sector, revenue, the scope of indemnity clauses with key counterparties, contract-breach history and contract-management systems. The exact premium and acceptance are confirmed after underwriting by insurers such as AIG, Chubb, DB, Hyundai, KB and Meritz.
