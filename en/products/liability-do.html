Overview

Directors & Officers Liability (D&O) covers a company's directors, auditors and executive officers for legal liability and defense costs when they face damages claims — from the company, shareholders, employees, counterparties or regulators — arising from breaches of the duty of care or fiduciary duty in the course of their duties.

It responds to directors' liability under the Commercial Act (Art. 399 / 401), disclosure liability under the Capital Markets Act, and derivative suits or regulatory investigations relating to fair-trade, tax and environmental law.

Key features

  • 01
    Side A / B / C structure

    Side A (paid directly to the individual) / Side B (reimbursement of company indemnification) / Side C (the company's own securities liability).

  • 02
    Defense-cost advancement

    Legal fees at the early stage of an investigation or suit are advanced promptly to support the officer's defense.

  • 03
    Extended cover for retired officers (ERP)

    An Extended Reporting Period of up to 6 years can be designed for acts during a retired officer's tenure.

  • 04
    Essential for listed / IPO companies

    Effectively required for listed companies, IPO candidates and PEF-invested companies.

Who needs it

  • 01
    Listed / IPO-track companies

    Listed and pre-IPO companies exposed to disclosure liability and securities class actions.

  • 02
    Unlisted mid-size / SMEs

    Risk from shareholder disputes, tax audits, FTC investigations and derivative suits.

  • 03
    Companies appointing outside directors / auditors

    D&O cover is a practical condition for appointing outside directors and auditors.

  • 04
    Start-ups raising capital

    A D&O requirement is commonly included in key SHA clauses on VC / PEF investment.

Main losses covered

  • Civil damages claims by shareholders, the company or third parties (including derivative suits and securities class actions)
  • Defense costs for criminal, administrative and regulatory investigations (legal and expert fees)
  • Costs of responding to investigations by the FTC, FSS, NTS and others
  • Claims by creditors or trustees in bankruptcy / rehabilitation proceedings
  • An officer's personal liability in employment-related suits (wrongful dismissal, discrimination, harassment)

Main endorsements

  • Subsidiary Coverage
  • Crisis Management costs
  • Reputation Protection costs
  • Extended Reporting Period (ERP)
  • Side A DIC (Difference-in-Conditions)

Main exclusions

  • Confirmed deliberate dishonesty, fraud, embezzlement or breach of trust by an officer
  • Disgorgement of profit gained from illegal personal benefit
  • Liability aggravated by contract
  • Insured vs. Insured suits (with some exceptions)
  • Bodily injury / property damage (covered separately by CGL, etc.)
  • ERISA / pension-related claims (without the relevant endorsement)

  • Audited reports / financial statements for the last 3 years

Other notes

  • As a Claims-Made contract, managing the retroactive date is very important.
  • A separate run-off contract should be reviewed around an M&A or IPO.
  • Updating the officer list is recommended when officers change or are newly appointed.

Cooling-off / duty of disclosure

  • Cooling-off (cancellation) is available within 15 days of receiving the certificate (excluding professional financial consumers).
  • If the duty to explain material matters is breached or a signature is missing, the contract may be cancelled within 3 months.
  • Breach of the duty of disclosure on application questions or financial information may lead to cancellation or reduced indemnity.
