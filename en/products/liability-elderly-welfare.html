Elderly Welfare Facility Liability Insurance

Covers liability for incidents in the course of care at elderly medical-welfare facilities (nursing homes, nursing hospitals, geriatric hospitals), home-care facilities (day/night care, short-stay, visiting care) and residential-welfare facilities — resident falls, pressure ulcers, food poisoning, infectious disease, transfer accidents and more. Recommended under the Social Welfare Services Act and the Long-Term Care Insurance Act.

Overview

Elderly welfare facilities have a high frequency and severity of incidents due to residents' physical and mental vulnerability. This cover comprehensively addresses resident bodily injury, facility-defect incidents and staff-negligence incidents during operation. Many local authorities require proof of cover as a condition of long-term-care licensing.

Three things easily missed when buying elderly welfare facility liability cover

The wording and structure points operators most often overlook

  • 1

    Checking fall-accident exclusions closely

    Falls are a core cover area, but exclusion definitions — resident's own fault, sub-standard facility safety, gross negligence — differ by wording. Comparing insurers' fall-related exclusions at application is essential, and the care-staff ratio and CCTV retention period directly affect the process.

  • 2

    Liability assessment for dementia / cognitive-decline patients

    For dementia or cognitive-decline patients, own-fault assessment differs from ordinary residents and the operator's protection and observation duty is assessed more strictly. Without combining a separate endorsement for disappearance and self-harm, gaps can arise.

  • 3

    Facility safety-standard grade and underwriting terms

    The safety-standard grade under the Elderly Welfare Act and Long-Term Care Insurance Act (fire, structure, staffing, CCTV) directly affects underwriting terms. Below standard, terms become conservative — limited cover, higher deductibles — so managing renewals and certifications also helps cut costs.

Frequently asked questions

The questions asked most when considering elderly welfare facility liability cover

Is elderly welfare facility liability insurance mandatory?

The insurance itself is not statutorily compulsory, but many local authorities effectively require proof of cover as a condition of facility licensing or long-term-care-institution designation under the Elderly Welfare Act and Long-Term Care Insurance Act. Given the high accident risk and compensation scale, it is close to essential in practice.

What incidents are covered?

Care-process incidents such as resident falls, fractures, pressure ulcers and medication misuse, meal food poisoning, transfer accidents (wheelchair, vehicle), facility-defect incidents and staff-negligence incidents are usually covered. Fire and natural disasters fall under a separate wording, usually combined by design.

How far are fall accidents covered?

Falls during care are a core cover area. But exclusions vary by wording — (1) a resident's clear own fault, (2) a situation the resident could medically control, (3) an incident below safety standards where the operator's gross negligence is recognised — so proving the inspection records and the protection state at the time is central to the process.

How is own-fault handled for dementia / cognitive-decline patients?

Dementia or cognitive-decline patients are treated differently from ordinary residents in own-fault apportionment. Whether the operator breached its protection and observation duty is the key item, and disappearance and self-harm are usually covered via a separate combined endorsement.

Are staff-abuse / wilful cases covered too?

Wilful abuse and criminal cases by staff are usually excluded. However, the operator's vicarious liability for breach of hiring, training, management and supervision duties can be assessed per the wording, so combining a separate endorsement to protect the operator should be considered.

Is cover refused if the facility is below safety standards?

Being below the safety standards under the Elderly Welfare Act and Long-Term Care Insurance Act (fire, structure, staffing ratio, CCTV) is unfavourable at underwriting and often makes terms conservative — limited cover, higher deductibles, specific-incident exclusions. The higher the safety grade, the better the terms.

How is the premium calculated?

The insurer calculates it based on facility type (nursing home, nursing hospital, day/night care, home care), number of residents, staffing ratio, safety-standard grade and past incident history. The exact premium and acceptance are confirmed after underwriting by insurers such as AIG, Chubb, DB, Hyundai, KB and Meritz.
