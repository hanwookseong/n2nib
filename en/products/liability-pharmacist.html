Overview

Under Articles 24 and 27 of the Pharmaceutical Affairs Act, pharmacists have a duty of accurate dispensing and medication guidance. This is a package combining ① professional liability (dispensing errors, missed medication guidance), ② premises liability (in-pharmacy incidents) and ③ PL (wrong OTC sales).

When you need pharmacist & pharmacy liability insurance

Common risk patterns in pharmacy operation — a five-scenario self-check

💊

Pharmacies dispensing on prescription

Errors in dispensing — ingredient, dose, instructions — directly affect patient health and can lead to liability.

🗣️

Pharmacists giving medication guidance

If a missed or erroneous medication guidance causes patient loss, the pharmacist's professional liability can arise.

🛒

Pharmacies selling OTC medicines

If a wrong OTC sale causes consumer loss, a product-liability (PL) type of responsibility can arise.

🏥

When running a hospital/nursing-home or herbal pharmacy

Pharmacies within a medical institution and herbal pharmacies have different prescription/dispensing environments and need cover matched to the risk.

🏬

Operating premises with patients and customers coming and going

Incidents from the premises themselves — in-pharmacy falls and slips, facility fire — are also within the liability risk.

A dispute pattern seen in the field

Pharmacist and pharmacy liability does not address a single risk. It is usually a package bundling three different risks — “professional liability” for dispensing errors and missed medication guidance, “premises liability” for in-pharmacy falls and slips, and “product liability (PL)” for wrong OTC sales. Because the cover that applies depends on “which risk” an incident falls under, it is important to confirm that your pharmacy is covered across all three. Acts breaching statutory requirements — wilful conduct or dispensing without a licence — fall within the exclusions.

Three things easily missed when buying pharmacist & pharmacy liability insurance

The wording and structure points decision-makers most often overlook

  • 1

    Professional, premises and PL are three different risks

    A pharmacy's risk divides into dispensing negligence (professional), in-pharmacy incidents (premises) and OTC sales (PL). Check that the package covers all three.

  • 2

    Unlicensed dispensing and wilful acts are excluded

    Loss from dispensing without a pharmacist's licence or from wilful acts is not covered. Separately from insurance, the requirements of the Pharmaceutical Affairs Act must be met.

  • 3

    For chains and multi-store, check the cover scope

    The insured scope and limit differ by operation — hospital pharmacies, chain head offices. Check that every store and pharmacist you operate is within cover.

Frequently asked questions

The questions asked most when considering pharmacist & pharmacy liability insurance

What risks does pharmacist & pharmacy liability insurance cover?

Based on the duty of accurate dispensing and medication guidance under Articles 24 and 27 of the Pharmaceutical Affairs Act, it is a package covering dispensing errors and missed guidance (professional), in-pharmacy falls and slips (premises), and wrong OTC sales (PL).

Are all dispensing errors covered?

Loss caused to a patient by dispensing negligence — ingredient, dose, instructions — is covered. However, loss from wilful acts or dispensing without a pharmacist's licence is excluded.

Is a customer falling inside the pharmacy covered too?

Incidents from the premises themselves, such as in-pharmacy falls and slips, can be covered under premises liability. Check that the package includes premises liability.

Is a wrong OTC sale covered too?

Where a wrong OTC sale causes consumer loss, it is handled by product-liability (PL) cover. Check that the package includes PL.

Can chains or attached pharmacies be covered?

Besides general pharmacies, hospital/nursing-home pharmacies, herbal pharmacies and chain head offices can be covered. The insured scope and limit differ by operation, so check that every store and pharmacist you operate is within cover.

How is the premium calculated?

The insurer calculates it based on pharmacy type and size, average monthly prescriptions, the risk scope covered (professional, premises, PL) and limit, and past claims history. The exact premium and terms are confirmed after underwriting by insurers such as AIG, Chubb, DB, Hyundai, KB and Meritz.
