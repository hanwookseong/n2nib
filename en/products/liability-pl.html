Overview

Product Liability Insurance (PL) covers the insured's legal liability under the Product Liability Act or the Civil Act when a defect in a product they manufactured, sold, supplied or repaired causes bodily injury or property damage to a third party.

A PL certificate is often a contractual requirement in supply (vendor) agreements with large distributors and global buyers, and in export contracts.

Key features

  • 01
    Product Liability Act (no-fault)

    Covers the no-fault liability under Korea's PL Act (in force since 2002) for manufacturing, design and labeling defects.

  • 02
    Domestic PL / Export PL

    Overseas PL endorsements (US, EU, Japan, etc.) can be added according to your export mix.

  • 03
    Litigation & defense costs included

    Beyond damages, lawyer fees and court costs are paid within the limit.

  • 04
    Linked recall endorsement

    The cost of recalling and disposing of defective products can be extended via a Product Recall endorsement.

Who needs it

  • 01
    Manufacturers

    All manufacturing sectors — food, electronics, machinery, chemicals, medical devices, auto parts.

  • 02
    Importers / distributors / sellers

    Under the PL Act, importers and distributors bear the same liability as manufacturers for defective products.

  • 03
    OEM / ODM suppliers

    Large principals often require a PL certificate to be submitted.

Main losses covered

  • Third-party bodily injury from a product defect (death, treatment costs, damages, etc.)
  • Third-party property damage from a product defect (damage or loss of property)
  • Indirect loss (e.g. business interruption) to the extent recognized by the court
  • Litigation, arbitration and lawyer costs borne by the insured
  • Emergency first-aid and loss-prevention costs

Main endorsements

  • Export PL endorsement (designed by region — US, EU, Japan, etc.)
  • Product Recall endorsement — recall and disposal of defective products
  • Vendor (additional-insured) endorsement for manufacturers / suppliers
  • Extended insured definition (ESR) endorsement
  • Own-product damage extension (some self-product loss included)

Main exclusions

  • Deliberate unlawful acts by the insured
  • Liability aggravated by contract (warranty, after-sales service, etc.)
  • Damage to the product itself (without the recall endorsement)
  • Products released while knowing of a foreseeable defect
  • Infringement of IP rights — patents, copyrights, trademarks
  • Nuclear risk, war, pollution (separate insurance)

Other notes

  • A Claims-Made contract covers only claims notified during the period, so the retroactive date must be managed.
  • Export PL premiums vary widely by the litigation environment of each country (especially the US).
  • Recall costs are excluded from the base cover — review the endorsement if needed.

Cooling-off / duty of disclosure

  • Cooling-off (cancellation) is available within 15 days of receiving the certificate (excluding professional financial consumers).
  • If the duty to explain material matters is breached or a signature is missing, the contract may be cancelled within 3 months.
  • Breach of the duty of disclosure may lead to cancellation or reduced indemnity.
