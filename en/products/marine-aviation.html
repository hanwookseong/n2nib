Why the surrender value may be less than the premiums paid, or nil

The surrender value is the amount paid if the contract is cancelled early. Unlike bank savings, insurance combines risk protection and savings: part of the premium is paid out as claims to other policyholders who suffer accidents, and part covers the insurer's operating expenses, so the surrender value on early cancellation may be less than the premiums paid, or nil.

A dispute pattern seen in the field

Aviation insurance starts from understanding it as two strands. One is the liability insurance an air-transport operator/operator must hold by "duty" under the Aviation Business Act and the ICAO/Montreal Convention — legal liability for passenger death/injury, ground third parties and carried cargo. The other is the "voluntary" hull insurance (Hull) covering loss to the aircraft itself. Aviation insurance is designed on English-language wordings (AVN/LSW), and one point is most often missed here: war, terrorism and aircraft hijacking are excluded under the base wording (AVN48B) and are covered only with a separate war-risk endorsement (such as AVN52) attached. Flight outside the authorised area, or operation by an unauthorised pilot, is also within the exclusions.

Three things easily missed when buying aviation insurance

The wording and structure points decision-makers most often overlook

  • 1

    Distinguishing compulsory liability + voluntary hull

    Aviation insurance splits into the statutorily compulsory liability insurance (passenger/third-party/cargo) and the voluntary hull insurance (Hull). Check that both areas are designed for your operation.

  • 2

    War/terrorism/hijack is a separate endorsement — base-excluded

    War, terrorism and aircraft hijack are excluded under the base wording (AVN48B). They are covered only with a separate war-risk endorsement, so check against the operating routes.

  • 3

    Flight outside the authorised area / unauthorised pilot is excluded

    An accident during flight outside the authorised area or operation by an unauthorised pilot is excluded. Compliance with operating rules is the premise of cover.

Frequently asked questions

The questions decision-makers ask most when considering aviation insurance

What is aviation insurance?

It comprehensively covers the loss risk of aircraft operation, combining the statutorily compulsory liability insurance (passenger, third-party, cargo) and the voluntary aircraft hull insurance (Hull).

Is aviation insurance compulsory?

Under the Aviation Business Act and international conventions such as the ICAO and Montreal Convention, an air-transport operator or aircraft operator has a duty to hold liability insurance. It is distinct from voluntary hull insurance.

Is loss from war or terrorism covered?

War, terrorism and aircraft hijacking are excluded under the base wording (AVN48B). They are covered only with a separate war-risk endorsement (such as AVN52) attached, so confirm against the operating routes.

What losses are covered?

It covers hull accidents in flight, take-off/landing and on the ground, legal liability for passenger death/injury, third-party bodily/property loss on the ground, carried cargo/mail loss, and salvage/debris-removal costs.

In what cases is cover limited?

Intent/gross-negligence accidents, flight outside the authorised area, operation by an unauthorised pilot, operation under alcohol/drugs and exceeding the durability limit through gradual wear are excluded.

How is the premium assessed?

It is advised in the insurer's underwriting process on the aircraft type and value, the routes and annual flight hours, pilot experience, maintenance history, the cover composition (hull/liability) and limit, and past incident history.
