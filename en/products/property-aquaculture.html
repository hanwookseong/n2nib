Why the surrender value may be less than the premiums paid, or nil

The surrender value is the amount paid if the contract is cancelled early. Unlike bank savings, insurance combines risk protection and savings: part of the premium is paid out as claims to other policyholders who suffer accidents, and part covers the insurer's operating expenses, so the surrender value on early cancellation may be less than the premiums paid, or nil.

A dispute pattern seen in the field

A cage farm on the south coast suffered cage-facility damage and the loss of many farmed fish when a typhoon struck. Under this wording, the cage facility and farmed-fish loss were reviewed, and proving the wording's wind-speed trigger (e.g. 25 m/s or more) with Meteorological Administration data became the key to the cover process. Additional death from a simultaneous red tide produced a large cover difference depending on whether a separate endorsement was combined. Marine risk has the characteristic that multiple risks combine even in a single accident — a case showing that site-by-site risk analysis plus an endorsement-combination design is essential.

Three things easily missed when buying aquaculture insurance

The wording and structure points decision-makers most often overlook

  • 1

    The wind-speed/wave trigger

    The wind-speed trigger for typhoon cover (e.g. 20–30 m/s) differs by wording. If official Meteorological Administration data falls below the wording threshold, cover does not operate, so confirm in advance that wind-speed measurement at the site matches the wording trigger.

  • 2

    Combining a red-tide / high-temperature endorsement

    Death from red tide, green tide and high water temperature is often excluded under the base wording and needs a separate endorsement. Choosing the endorsement to match the site's marine-environment risk is key to preventing a cover gap.

  • 3

    The shipping cycle and the BI design

    The shipping cycle of farmed fish is 1–3 years, so a single accident affects sales over several years. The 12-month indemnity of an ordinary BI wording cannot keep up with the cycle, so an aquaculture BI endorsement or a long indemnity period is recommended.

Frequently asked questions

The questions decision-makers ask most when considering aquaculture insurance

What does aquaculture insurance cover?

It covers loss where farmed fish, shellfish or seaweed die or are lost through natural disaster, disease or accident. It combines cover for the farmed assets, the facilities (cages, land-based facilities) and business interruption.

What accidents are covered?

Typhoon, tidal wave and sea-temperature change, marine-environment accidents such as red tide and green tide, damage to farming facilities (cages, land-based tanks), some fish diseases (some statutory aquatic-animal epidemics), and rearing-environment accidents from power failure are the usual cover areas.

How do natural-disaster exclusions apply?

Storm-flood such as typhoon, tidal wave and strong wind is usually covered, but the wind-speed/wave trigger differs by wording. Marine-environment changes such as red tide and high water temperature often need a separate endorsement, so choosing the endorsement to match the site's marine risk is key.

Are fish diseases and epidemics covered?

Statutory aquatic-animal epidemics (VHS, KHV and so on) vary in cover scope by wording. Confirm in advance the overlap/complement between government disposal compensation and this wording, and an endorsement for loss beyond government compensation is recommended.

How are the farming facilities covered?

The nets, buoys and anchor facilities of cage farms and the tanks and filtration facilities of land-based farms are included in the facility-cover area, separate from the farmed assets. Since a single accident can damage facilities and farmed fish together, balancing the limits of the two areas is essential.

Is business-interruption loss covered?

Farmed fish have a long shipping cycle (usually 1–3 years), so a single accident can affect sales over several years. Business-interruption loss can be combined through a separate BI endorsement, and designing an indemnity period that matches the shipping cycle is recommended.

How is the premium assessed?

The insurer assesses it on the species, scale and value of the farmed stock, the site location (sea, inland water, land), the natural-disaster risk grade, the facility condition, past death history and whether a government subsidy applies. The exact premium and whether cover can be accepted are confirmed after underwriting by insurers such as AIG, Chubb, DB, KB, Meritz and Hyundai.
