Overview

Contingency insurance that transfers the large-prize payout risk of a prize, draw or promotion event to a fixed premium, making the marketing budget predictable.

Key features

  • 01
    Fixed premium, uncertain-risk transfer

    A fixed premium set by statistically calculating the win probability.

  • 02
    Various event types

    Sports events, lottery-style draws, TV quizzes, SNS events.

  • 03
    Short / one-off enrolment

    A short contract of days to months, matched to the event.

  • 04
    Maximised marketing impact

    A large prize staked while the actual outlay is capped at the premium.

Who needs it

  • 01
    Sports / entertainment event planners

    In-game events, concert draws.

  • 02
    Retail / franchise head offices

    Seasonal promotions and opening draws.

  • 03
    Broadcasters / advertisers

    TV-programme quizzes, SNS viral campaigns.

Main losses covered

  • Prize/gift value the insured must pay on meeting the event condition
  • Gift purchase/procurement cost (gift-in-kind type)
  • Tax/levy payment (endorsement possible)
  • Event-verifier cost (some)

Special endorsements (additional cover)

  • Tax/levy-payment endorsement
  • Limit extension for multiple winners
  • Cash prize in place of a gift in kind
  • Event cancellation/postponement linkage

Losses not covered (main exclusions)

  • A win by rule breach or dishonesty
  • Intent/collusion of the insured, staff or agency
  • Payment despite incomplete win condition/evidence
  • A law-breaching or gambling-type event

Why the surrender value may be less than the premiums paid, or nil

The surrender value is the amount paid if the contract is cancelled early. Unlike bank savings, insurance combines risk protection and savings: part of the premium is paid out as claims to other policyholders who suffer accidents, and part covers the insurer's operating expenses, so the surrender value on early cancellation may be less than the premiums paid, or nil.

Depositor protection

  • (However, a policy whose policyholder and premium payer is a corporation is not protected.)

Situations that come up often at a marketing event — a five-scenario self-check

🎤

📺

💰

Wanting to stake a large prize but control actual outlay

A large prize boosts marketing while the actual outlay is capped at the set premium.

A dispute pattern seen in the field

The principle of prize/contingency insurance is "turning an uncertain large risk into a fixed premium". When a marketing event stakes a condition such as a large cash prize for a successful half-court shot, the probability of meeting it is low, but if met a large sum must be paid — an uncertain risk. This insurance sets a fixed premium by statistically calculating the win probability and covers the prize/gift value the insured must pay when the condition is actually met. So a large prize can boost the marketing effect while the actual outlay is controlled at the set-premium level. But the event condition and running rules must be clear and fair for cover to operate.

Three things easily missed when buying prize / contingency insurance

The wording and structure points decision-makers most often overlook

  • 1

    Clarity of the event condition and running rules

    If the win condition and running rules are unclear, they become a ground for dispute on an incident. Define the condition clearly for anyone to read.

  • 2

    The premise of probability calculation — fair running

    The premium is set by statistically calculating the win probability. If the event runs differently from that premise, cover can be affected.

  • 3

    Short/one-off — exactly match the event period

    This is usually a short contract matched to the event. Check that the policy period fully includes the actual event period.

Frequently asked questions

The questions decision-makers ask most when considering prize indemnity / contingency insurance

What is prize / contingency insurance?

It covers the prize or gift value the insured must pay when a high, pre-undetermined prize arises in a marketing or promotional event.

How can a large prize be met by premium alone?

The premium is set as a fixed amount by statistically calculating the win probability. A large prize can boost the marketing effect while the actual outlay is controlled at the set-premium level.

Which events can use it?

It can be used for various event types — in-game sports events, lottery-style draws, TV quizzes, SNS viral campaigns.

Can a short event be covered?

A short-term contract of a few days to a few months, matched to the event period, is possible. Check that the policy period fully includes the actual event period.

What if the event rules are ambiguous?

If the win condition and running rules are not clear, it becomes a ground for dispute. Define the condition clearly for anyone to read, and ensure it does not run differently from the assessment premise.

How is the premium assessed?

It is advised in the insurer's underwriting process on the event type and win condition, the win probability, the prize/gift value and the event scale and period.
