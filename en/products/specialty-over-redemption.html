Why the surrender value may be less than the premiums paid, or nil

The surrender value is the amount paid if the contract is cancelled early. Unlike bank savings, insurance combines risk protection and savings: part of the premium is paid out as claims to other policyholders who suffer accidents, and part covers the insurer's operating expenses, so the surrender value on early cancellation may be less than the premiums paid, or nil.

The cost-overrun risk when a campaign is "too successful" can be controlled with insurance.

A dispute pattern seen in the field

Over-redemption insurance addresses a somewhat paradoxical risk: the cost risk that arises not because a marketing campaign "fails" but because it is "too successful". Prize, coupon and mileage promotions set a budget by estimating the response and redemption rate in advance; if the campaign is far more successful than expected, payout exceeding that budget arises. This insurance covers that excess — excess prize/coupon costs, concentrated mileage redemption, large-winner costs and emergency replacement-prize procurement. Because the excess threshold (trigger) is set on the basis of response/redemption statistics, submitting accurate prior statistics is the premise.

Three things easily missed when buying over-redemption insurance

The wording and structure points decision-makers most often overlook

  • 1

    Submitting prior statistics and budget is the premise

    The excess threshold is set on response/redemption statistics. Failing to submit accurate prior statistics and budget data affects cover.

  • 2

    A deliberately over-designed promotion or manipulated participation is excluded

    Excess from a deliberately over-designed promotion or from fraudulent/manipulated participation is not covered.

  • 3

    The accuracy of the trigger (excess threshold)

    Cover operates when the pre-set excess threshold (trigger) is passed. Check that the trigger is set to match the campaign's actual budget structure.

Frequently asked questions

The questions decision-makers ask most when considering over-redemption insurance

What is over-redemption insurance?

It covers the excess where a marketing campaign is more successful than expected and the prize, coupon or mileage payout exceeds the budget set in advance.

Why is a successful campaign a risk?

Prize, coupon and mileage promotions set a budget by estimating the response and redemption rate in advance. If the campaign is far more successful than expected, payout exceeding that budget arises, becoming a cost risk.

Which costs are covered?

It covers prize/coupon payout exceeding the budget, concentrated mileage redemption, large-winner costs, and emergency replacement-prize procurement costs.

In what cases is cover limited?

Exclusions include a deliberately over-designed promotion, fraudulent or manipulated participation, delayed wording change, and failure to submit the prior statistics.

How is the cover threshold set?

An excess threshold (trigger) is set on the basis of response/redemption-rate statistics. Check that the trigger is set to match the campaign's actual budget structure.

How is the premium assessed?

It is advised in the insurer's underwriting process on the campaign type and budget scale, past response/redemption rates, the prize/coupon structure and the excess-threshold setting.
